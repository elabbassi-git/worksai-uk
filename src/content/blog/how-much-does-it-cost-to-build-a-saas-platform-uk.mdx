## Why "how much?" is the wrong first question

Asking what a SaaS platform costs is like asking what a house costs. A studio flat and a five-bedroom detached are both "a house". Any honest answer starts with **what you need built**. The good news is that the cost drivers are well understood, so you can control them.

### 1. The scope of your first release

Every screen, form and workflow takes design, build and testing time. The single best way to control cost is to launch with **one core job done well**, then add features once real customers tell you what matters.

### 2. The number of user roles

A platform where one type of user does one thing is simple. Add staff, admins, managers and your customers' own customers, each with different permissions, and complexity grows quickly because every screen has to respect every role.

### 3. Integrations

Each connection to another system, such as Stripe, Xero, a CRM or a government API, has to be built, tested and handled when it fails. Well-documented APIs are quicker; older or poorly documented systems take longer.

### 4. Data, security and compliance

Multi-tenant isolation, audit logs, data retention rules and UK GDPR requirements are essential for most B2B platforms. Regulated sectors, such as finance or health, add further review and documentation.

### 5. Design depth

A clean, consistent interface built on a solid design system is efficient. Highly bespoke animation and illustration across every screen costs more. Both can look excellent; the difference is effort.

### 6. AI features

Search, document reading and assistants can transform a product, but they need careful testing against real examples and safeguards for when the model is unsure. We cover this in our [AI web app development](/services/ai-web-development/) service.

### 7. Who builds it

A solo freelancer, a studio and a large consultancy all price differently. What matters most is whether the price is **fixed against a clear scope**, and whether you own the result.

## Build cost vs running cost

Keep these two numbers separate:

- **Build cost:** designing, engineering, testing and launching the platform. At Works AI this is a single, one-time payment.
- **Running cost:** hosting, database, email sending, payment fees and any AI usage. These are paid directly to the providers, in accounts in your name, and scale with your usage. A good studio estimates them for you before you commit.

## The bottom line

The cost of a SaaS platform is driven by what you ask it to do. Keep the first release focused, insist on a fixed price against a clear scope, own your code and accounts, and separate build cost from running costs.
