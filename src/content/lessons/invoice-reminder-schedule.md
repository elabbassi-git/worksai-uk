Before you automate anything, you need a plan for **what** to send, **when**, and **when to stop**. Automation only repeats your plan perfectly. A badly planned schedule, automated, just annoys customers faster. This lesson helps you design a schedule you would be happy to send by hand.

## Start with your payment terms

Your reminders should match the terms printed on your invoices. Check three things:

1. **Payment period:** for example, 14 or 30 days from the invoice date.
2. **How to pay:** bank transfer details, card link or both.
3. **What happens if payment is late:** whether you charge interest, pause work or both.

In the UK, businesses can usually claim statutory interest and fixed compensation on late payments from other businesses. Whether you choose to mention or apply this is a business decision; if in doubt, ask your accountant.

## The five-step schedule

Most small businesses do well with a schedule like this. Adjust the days to suit your terms and customers.

| When | Tone | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 3 days before due | Friendly | A heads-up with the invoice attached |
| On the due date | Friendly | A short reminder with a pay-now link |
| 7 days late | Polite | "Just checking this hasn't been missed" |
| 14 days late | Firm | Refers to your payment terms |
| 21 days late | Personal | Alert to **you** to phone the customer |

Notice the last step is not another automatic message. After two or three reminders, a phone call from a real person is usually more effective, and it protects the relationship.

## Writing the messages

Keep each message short. Include the invoice number, amount, due date and a way to pay in one click. Write them in your own voice. For example, a 7-days-late reminder:

> Hi Sam, a quick note that invoice 1043 for £420 was due on 12 November. If it's already on its way, thank you, please ignore this. If not, you can pay here: [link]. Any questions, just reply.

Avoid threatening language in early reminders. Most late payers are disorganised, not dishonest.

## Decide who should not get automatic reminders

Make a short exclusion list: your biggest clients, customers on payment plans, anyone in a dispute. These get a personal approach. In the full course you will build this list into the automation so they are skipped automatically.

## When to stop

The automation must stop the moment an invoice is paid. This sounds obvious, but it is the most common failure in home-made systems: a customer pays and then receives a "final reminder". In the course, the workflow checks the invoice status in Xero before every single message.

## Your homework

Write your five messages and choose your timings. Show them to someone you trust and ask: would you be annoyed to receive this? Once you are happy, you are ready to build.
