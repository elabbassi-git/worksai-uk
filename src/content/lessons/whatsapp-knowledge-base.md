The single biggest factor in whether a WhatsApp AI assistant is helpful or embarrassing is not the AI model. It is the **knowledge base**: the information you give it to answer from. In this lesson you will write a first version for your own business. You don't need any software yet, just a document.

## Why the knowledge base matters more than the AI

Modern AI models are fluent. They will always produce a confident-sounding answer. If you haven't told the assistant your prices, it may guess. If you haven't told it which areas you cover, it may say yes to a customer 60 miles away. A good knowledge base does two things: it gives the assistant the facts, and it tells it clearly what to do when the facts run out.

## Step 1: Collect the questions people actually ask

Open your WhatsApp, email and voicemail from the last month. Write down every question customers asked, in their words. Most businesses find the same 15 to 30 questions cover the large majority of messages. Group them, for example:

- **Services:** "Do you do...?", "Can you...?"
- **Prices:** "How much is...?", "Do you charge for call-outs?"
- **Availability:** "Are you free on Saturday?", "How soon can you come?"
- **Area:** "Do you cover...?"
- **Practical:** parking, what to bring, how to pay, cancellation

## Step 2: Write short, factual answers

For each question, write the answer as you would say it to a customer, in two or three sentences. Be specific. "Prices start from £60 for a standard service; we confirm the exact price before we start" is better than "Prices vary".

Keep one fact in one place. If your call-out charge appears in five answers and you change it, you will forget one. Put it in a **Prices** section and refer to it.

## Step 3: Write the rules, not just the facts

This is the part most people skip. Add a section called **Rules** and write, in plain sentences, how the assistant must behave. For example:

1. Always say you are an AI assistant for [business name].
2. Only answer from the information in this document. If the answer isn't here, say you will pass the question to the team.
3. Never give a fixed price for work that needs a visit. Offer to book a visit instead.
4. If someone mentions a leak near electrics, gas, or anything dangerous, tell them to call [number] now and alert the team.
5. If the customer is unhappy or asks for a person, hand over straight away.

These rules are what make an assistant safe to leave running overnight.

## Step 4: Add your tone of voice

Write three or four lines describing how you talk to customers: friendly or formal, first names or not, emojis or not. Then include two example replies you have actually sent that you were happy with. Examples teach tone far better than adjectives do.

## Step 5: Test it on paper

Before any software is involved, ask a friend to play a customer. They send you questions; you answer **only** using the document. Every time you have to make something up, you have found a gap. Add it. Every time the right answer is "I'll ask the team", check there is a rule that says so.

## A simple structure to copy

- **About us:** one paragraph on what you do and where
- **Services:** one short section per service
- **Prices:** all prices in one place, with what's included
- **Area and hours**
- **Booking:** how booking works and what details you need
- **Policies:** cancellation, payment, guarantees
- **Rules:** how the assistant must behave and when to hand over
- **Tone:** description plus two example replies
