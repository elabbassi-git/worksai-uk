Of everything on your Google Business Profile, your **primary category** has one of the biggest effects on which searches you appear for. Many businesses choose it in a hurry during set-up and never look at it again. This lesson shows you how to choose it properly, in about fifteen minutes.

## What categories do

Categories tell Google what kind of business you are. When someone searches "emergency plumber" or "nail salon near me", Google looks for businesses whose categories match that kind of search. You get **one primary category** and can add **secondary categories** for other things you genuinely do.

Categories also switch on features. Some categories show a "Book" button, a menu, or specific attributes. Picking the right one changes what your profile can display.

## Step 1: Describe your business in three words

Before you open Google, write down how a customer would describe you in three words or fewer. Not your brand, not your slogan: "electrician", "dog groomer", "accountant". This is your starting point for the primary category.

## Step 2: Search like a customer

Search Google for your main service plus your town, for example "electrician Leicester". Look at the businesses in the map results. Their main category appears under their name. Note which categories the top results use. You are looking for the most **specific** category that accurately describes your core business.

Specific beats general. "Electrician" is better than "Contractor". "Indian restaurant" is better than "Restaurant". "Family law attorney" is better than "Lawyer" (Google uses some American terms, so check what's available).

## Step 3: Set your primary category

In your Google Business Profile, edit your business information and choose the primary category. Type slowly: the list suggests options as you go, and there are often several close variations. Pick the one that matches what **most of your revenue** comes from, not your newest or favourite service.

## Step 4: Add secondary categories carefully

Add secondary categories only for services you actively offer and want to be found for. An electrician who also installs EV chargers might add "Electric vehicle charging station contractor". Don't add categories for things you do once a year. Too many loosely related categories can blur what you are known for.

A reasonable number for most small businesses is between two and five in total.

## Step 5: Match your services and website

Categories work best when the rest of your profile agrees with them:

- Add **services** in your profile that match each category
- Make sure your **business description** mentions them naturally
- Have a **page on your website** for each main service

When your categories, services and website all say the same thing, Google has a much clearer picture of what you do.

## Common mistakes

- Choosing a broad category because it "covers everything"
- Putting a keyword or town name in your **business name**: this breaks Google's guidelines and can get your profile suspended
- Copying a competitor's categories without checking they fit you
- Changing the primary category frequently. Make a considered choice, then give it time
