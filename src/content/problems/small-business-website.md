## Why customers expect a website

When someone hears about you, from a friend, a van, a leaflet or a Google search, the next thing most of them do is look you up. If they find a clear, up-to-date website, it reassures them you are a real, active business. If they find nothing, or only an old directory listing, many simply move on to someone who looks more established.

A website also works when you don't. It answers the same questions at 10pm as at 10am, and it can take enquiries while you are busy.

## What a small business website actually needs

You don't need dozens of pages or clever animations. For most small businesses, five to eight pages are enough:

1. **Home:** what you do, where, and how to get in touch, in the first screen.
2. **One page per main service:** what's included, who it's for, rough prices or how pricing works, and common questions.
3. **About:** who you are, your experience and qualifications, real photos.
4. **Contact:** phone, email, WhatsApp, a short form, your areas and hours.
5. **Optional:** reviews, a gallery of work, FAQs, booking.

Each service page should be named the way customers search. "Boiler servicing in Bristol" works better than "Our Solutions".

## The mistakes that cost you enquiries

- **No phone number on mobile.** Most visitors are on a phone and want to tap to call.
- **Slow pages.** Large images and heavy themes make visitors leave before the page loads.
- **Vague wording.** "Quality service at competitive prices" says nothing. Say what you do and for whom.
- **No way to contact you out of hours.** A short form or an AI assistant can capture the lead.
- **Not telling Google.** A new site isn't found automatically. Search Console and a sitemap help Google discover it.
- **Someone else owns the domain.** Always register the domain in your own name.

## DIY, freelancer or agency?

**DIY builders** are cheapest and fine if you enjoy it and have the time. **Freelancers** vary a lot in quality, so check what happens after launch. **Agencies** cost more but should handle writing, SEO, hosting and support. Whichever you choose, ask who owns the site, what the monthly costs are, and what happens if you want to leave.
